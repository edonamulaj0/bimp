The problem

Site engineers spend hours every day searching drawing sets for one number or note. General-purpose AI tools guess when they read complex technical drawings — and a guess you can't check is worse than no answer.

How it works

  1. Upload a drawing set — PDF, vector or scanned, in any language.
  2. Ask a question in plain words.
  3. Get the answer with evidence — sheet, grid location and a highlighted crop you can verify in seconds.

Example questions

“What's the fire rating of door T1.04?”

Answered from the door schedule; the row is highlighted on the sheet.

“How many tiles for the level 2 bathroom?”

Room outline shown, area × tile size + 10% waste, formula and assumptions listed.

“How many windows on the north façade?”

Each counted window is marked; schedule and plan counts are compared.

“Which detail shows the parapet?”

The callout is followed to the detail sheet and opened there.

Why you can trust it

  • No source, no answer. Every claim cites a sheet and region. If nothing supports it, BIMP says “Not found in the drawings”.
  • Exact values from the drawing. Numbers come from extracted text, tables and geometry — not estimated by a model.
  • Quantities show their working. Inputs, formula, assumptions and result.
  • Conflicts are flagged. When two sheets disagree, you see both.

Formats and languages

PDF and image floor plans today, turned into a 2D plan and an isometric 3D model (labelled as a preview until measured). DWG/DXF and IFC are planned. Questions in any language; drawing text quoted as written.

Who it's for

Site engineers who need one number fast. Project managers checking quantities across sheets. Estimators who need take-offs they can defend.
